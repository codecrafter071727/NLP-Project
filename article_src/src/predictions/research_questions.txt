RQ1 official evaluation

Protocol
Rows are read from datastore/frjudge.csv.
Each row stores an original sentence, a simplification, and a human rating.
It does not store age, gender, birth region, education, English proficiency, or LLM experience.
Each characteristic is measured from the original sentence only.
Each formulation measure describes the simplification.
Correlations are estimated on the training split (n=186) and checked on the test split (n=90).
A link is reliable when the two correlations have the same sign and the test p-value is below 0.0025.
That threshold is 0.05 divided by the number of characteristic-formulation pairs, the Bonferroni correction.
The identical and unrelated holdouts are not used: their simplifications were constructed, not written from the source sentence.

RQ2 official evaluation

Protocol
The ridge model is trained only on the train split of datastore/frjudge.csv.
Dev, test, and both holdouts are scored after training.
The identical holdout is the original sentence copied as its own simplification, so every human label is 10.
The unrelated holdout pairs each original with an unrelated simplification, so every human label is 0.
Correlation is undefined when the human labels do not vary. Error is the measure on those holdouts.

RQ3 official evaluation

Protocol
Rows are read from datastore/frjudge.csv.
Each trait is measured from the original sentence and its simplification.
The same pair always produces the same trait value.
Correlation with the human rating is estimated on the training split (n=186) and checked on the test split (n=90).
A trait is reliable when three things hold: the identical holdout sits on the expected side of the unrelated holdout,
the training and test correlations have the same sign, and the test correlation has p < 0.05.
This dataset has no user search logs. The traits describe how a legal sentence was simplified.

Reliable traits: 4 of 7.
Reliable: overlap, words_kept, length_closeness, length_change.

What each trait measures
  overlap: Share of words that appear in both texts.
  words_kept: Share of original words that remain in the simplification.
  words_from_original: Share of simplification words that come from the original.
  length_closeness: 1 when the two texts have the same number of words, lower when one is much longer.
  length_change: Simplification length divided by original length. 1 means no change.
  sentence_split: Simplification sentences divided by original sentences. Above 1 means the sentence was split.
  shorter_words: Average original word length minus average simplification word length.
